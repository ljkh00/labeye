Final Year Project 2 · Presentation

Turning raw honeypot data into readable, classified attacks

A honeypot attack analysis system that captures real attacker activity with T-Pot, then reconstructs it into sessions, classifies each by attack type, and explains every event in plain language — so students and entry-level analysts can understand attacks without reading raw logs.

01Problem Statement why this project exists
The problem
  • Raw honeypot data is dense, technical JSON — hard for newcomers to read
  • Events are scattered, not grouped into coherent attacks
  • Expert dashboards (Kibana) overwhelm students & junior analysts
  • No simple layer sits between raw telemetry and the expert view
The impact
  • Learners can't turn raw records into a picture of what actually happened
  • Weak readiness for real SOC attack-analysis work
  • Little safe practice on genuine attacker data
In one line: the data exists, but it isn't readable to the people learning to analyse it.
02Research Questions & Objectives RQ → RO, paired
1
Research Question

What are the requirements for reconstructing and classifying honeypot attack data, and is it feasible to build a simplified analysis interface over T-Pot?

Objective

Identify the requirements and confirm technical feasibility of an analysis interface that consumes T-Pot telemetry.

2
Research Question

How can a honeypot system with a custom analysis interface be designed and implemented to help analysts reconstruct, classify, and interpret captured attacks?

Objective

Design and build a system that reconstructs sessions, classifies them by attack type, explains events, and presents them in a dashboard.

3
Research Question

How effective is the interface at improving usability and interpretation of attack data compared to the baseline Kibana dashboard?

Objective

Evaluate the interface with target users through functional testing and a semi-structured interview against Kibana.
